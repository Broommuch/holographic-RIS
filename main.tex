\begin{abstract}
Holographic metasurfaces, which use only reference-assisted radio-frequency (RF) energy measurements to recover complex-valued information from transmitted signals, offer a low-complexity, energy-efficient reception architecture for next-generation wireless systems. However, existing holographic receivers typically rely on coordinated measurements across antenna elements to form a spatial interference pattern, constraining aperture flexibility. This paper develops a reference-assisted phase retrieval framework for
holographic MU-SIMO reception, jointly addressing continuous-valued
channel parameter estimation and finite-alphabet symbol detection
using only per-element RF intensity measurements. A controllable
RF reference is superimposed element-wise with the incident field before
square-law detection, breaking the global-phase invariance without coherent
I/Q acquisition. For the resulting affine intensity model, we establish an
exact secant identity and derive Jacobian-based identifiability and stability
conditions for continuous channel parameters. For finite-alphabet signaling,
we show that noiseless symbol recovery is unique if and only if the minimum
energy-domain distance is positive, and derive corresponding pairwise-error and
stability bounds. A spatial four-phase reference code yields an average
separation guarantee and reveals that severe reference--signal energy imbalance
weakens phase information. Guided by these results, a two-phase receiver combines
linearized intensity-domain initialization, stability-guided adaptive training,
damped Gauss--Newton channel estimation, and distance-certified
Gerchberg--Saxton symbol detection. Simulations verify the theoretical
conditions and show that, at $10$~dB, the adaptive policy uses $20.0$ pilot
symbols on average instead of the fixed budget of $40$ while attaining a
channel normalized mean-square error (NMSE) of $-28.1$~dB; the proposed distance metric also predicts
finite-alphabet detection reliability.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Holographic MU-SIMO, reference-assisted phase retrieval, channel identifiability, finite-alphabet symbol recovery, intensity-only measurements.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Future sixth-generation (6G) wireless systems are expected to support
massive connectivity while improving spectral and energy efficiency\cite{hu2018data,dardari2020large}.
To meet these demands, electrically large receive apertures have been widely adopted, as they enhance spatial resolution and enable the separation of simultaneously transmitting users\cite{r2,NEholo}.
In this context, large, low-power intelligent surfaces extend conventional array processing to enable dynamic reconfiguration of the electromagnetic environment, attracting substantial research interest across surface control, direction finding, sensing, localization, and wavenumber-domain processing
\cite{huang2020holographic,zhang2021holographic,fuhai,li2026intelligent,cao2026hybrid,zhang2026wavenumber}.
However, acquiring in-phase and quadrature (I/Q) samples at every array element requires a coherent radio-frequency (RF) chain per element, driving up hardware complexity and power consumption.

Holographic metasurfaces with energy-detection front ends offer a different scaling path. Square-law detectors directly convert RF waveforms into low-rate real measurements and are well established in direct-detection communications \cite{kahn1997wireless,armstrong2009ofdm}; related RF architectures can reduce per-element acquisition overhead \cite{shen2020intelligent}. This simplification also changes what is available for subsequent processing: each detector reports energy rather than a complex field sample. The phase relationships needed to identify spatial channels, separate simultaneous users, and distinguish PSK and QAM symbols are therefore no longer directly available. For an energy-domain aperture to support these communication functions, those relationships must be encoded in the measured intensity pattern.

A known RF reference can bridge the gap between energy-only acquisition and
the phase information needed for communication. When the received field and
reference wave interfere before square-law detection, their cross term makes
the measured energy depend on relative phase. Spatially coding the reference
across the aperture produces a holographic intensity pattern that carries
information about the incident complex wavefront
\cite{gabor1948new,leith1962wavefronts,yamaguchi1997phase}. Because this
wavefront is shaped by the channels and transmitted symbols of concurrent
users, the same principle can support channel acquisition, user separation,
and data recovery. Reference-assisted energy-domain holographic reception thus
offers a route to large-aperture communication with low-rate real measurements
and without a coherent I/Q chain at every element.

Phaseless channel estimation has also been investigated in OFDM systems \cite{ofdm_phase_retrieval}. For holographic interference surfaces, phase-shifting interference suppression and Prony-based multiuser channel segmentation have been developed, with the channel-estimation error characterized as the number of surface elements increases \cite{huangjd2024CS}. Wideband channel recovery has
also been studied with a corresponding Cram\'er--Rao bound
\cite{huang2026wideband}, and a prototype has demonstrated physical feasibility
\cite{yin2025hisprototype}. These studies provide sensing algorithms,
performance analysis, and hardware evidence for reference-assisted reception\cite{Zhou2024,r1,r3}. However, conventional holographic receiver designs generally rely on coordinated measurements across antenna elements to construct spatial interference patterns, which inherently couples the operation of all elements and constrains aperture flexibility. A fundamental RF-domain understanding of how the received and reference fields interfere, and how the reference field, transmit waveforms, and array geometry jointly shape the information retained after square-law detection, remains limited.

This paper develops a two-phase framework for complex channel recovery and
finite-alphabet detection from reference-assisted RF energy measurements. A
common affine-intensity model connects the two tasks, while criteria matched to
their domains---secant and Jacobian conditions for continuous channel
parameters and an energy-domain minimum distance for discrete symbols---expose
the roles of pilot, aperture, and reference diversity. These criteria then
guide adaptive training, nonlinear channel refinement, and certified iterative
detection.

The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item We formulate reference-assisted energy-domain holographic reception as a
two-phase communication architecture. A spatially coded RF reference enables a
common affine-intensity model for complex channel acquisition and data
detection without coherent I/Q acquisition.
\item For continuous channel recovery, we establish an exact secant identity
and Jacobian-based uniqueness and stability bounds. The resulting parametric
rank and measurement-count requirements expose how pilot, aperture, and
reference diversity determine whether the physical channel can be reliably
estimated. This provides theoretical guidelines for configuring pilot length, array aperture, and reference coding so as to avoid ill-conditioned channel inversion and excessive training sequence overhead in practice.
\item For finite-alphabet detection, we characterize noiseless uniqueness by
an energy-domain minimum distance, derive pairwise and block-error bounds, and
quantify how spatial four-phase coding and reference--signal energy allocation
affect symbol separation. This yields an analytical basis for selecting constellation-compatible reference codes and balancing reference-to-signal energy to prevent phase-ambiguity-induced symbol errors.
\item Guided by these communication-level criteria, we develop a complete
two-phase receiver combining linearized initialization, stability-guided
adaptive training, damped Gauss--Newton channel estimation, and
distance-certified reference-assisted Gerchberg--Saxton detection. This enables practical, low-complexity multiuser reception without coherent I/Q chains while adapting pilot usage and early stopping to channel and noise conditions.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section~II presents the
reference-assisted RF holographic receiver and its unified training and data
models. Section~III establishes the uniqueness, stability, and finite-alphabet
reliability results. Section~IV develops the corresponding channel-estimation
and symbol-detection algorithms and derives the DOA CRLB. Section~V reports the
numerical results, and Section~VI concludes the paper.


\section{RF Holographic Reception Architecture and Signal Model}
\label{sec:system_model}

Optical holography records the interference of an unknown object wave with a known reference as an intensity pattern, typically to reconstruct the object's wavefront \cite{gabor1948new,leith1962wavefronts,yamaguchi1997phase}. RF holographic reception applies this principle to wireless signals by superposing a known reference wave with the unknown incident RF field prior to square-law detection \cite{huangjd2024CS,yin2025hisprototype}. Through spatial reference phase coding across the aperture, phase variations are converted into intensity measurements that support channel and data recovery without coherent downconversion or I/Q sampling at each element.

Fig.~\ref{fig:model} contrasts conventional coherent reception with the proposed reference-assisted energy-domain architecture. In conventional reception, each element passes through an RF front end, I/Q mixing, low-pass filtering, and I/Q ADC sampling, incurring high hardware complexity, power consumption, and cost. By contrast, the proposed scheme distributes a common RF reference with spatial phase coding and uses square-law detection followed by real-valued ADC sampling. Eliminating per-element coherent downconversion and complex I/Q sampling substantially reduces RF and ADC overhead as aperture size grows, shifting the burden of phase-sensitive channel and symbol recovery to physical reference coding and downstream signal processing. Scheduled users are assumed to be frame- and symbol-synchronous via standard uplink synchronization and timing control \cite{morelli2004timing,sanguinetti2009robust}, with negligible residual timing and carrier-frequency offsets over each detection interval.

\subsection{Transmitter Model}

The $K$ symbols transmitted by user $l$ in one signaling block are collected in $\mathbf{s}_l = [s_{l,0},\ldots,s_{l,K-1}]^T \in \mathcal{S}^K$, where $\mathcal{S} = \{c_0,\ldots,c_{M_{\rm mod}-1}\} \subset \mathbb{C}$ is an equiprobable constellation normalized to $\frac{1}{M_{\rm mod}}\sum_{a=0}^{M_{\rm mod}-1}|c_a|^2 = \mathbb{E}\{|s_{l,k}|^2\} = 1$. After pulse
shaping, the complex baseband waveform is
\begin{equation}
x_l(t)
=
\sqrt{P_l}
\sum_{k=0}^{K-1} s_{l,k} p(t-kT_s),
\label{eq:baseband_signal_l}
\end{equation}
Here, $P_l$ is the transmit power, $p(t)$ is the pulse-shaping filter, and
$T_s$ is the symbol duration.
The corresponding real waveform radiated by user $l$ is
\begin{equation}
x_l^{\rm RF}(t)
=
\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Re}\!\left\{x_l(t)e^{j2\pi f_ct}\right\},
\label{eq:passband_signal_l}
\end{equation}
where $f_c$ is the carrier frequency. Under the usual carrier-period
averaging, the factor $\sqrt{2}$ makes the average power of
$x_l^{\rm RF}(t)$ equal to the envelope power $|x_l(t)|^2$. Thus, $x_l(t)$
contains the pulse-shaped amplitude and phase information, while the carrier
term translates that waveform to RF for propagation. 

\subsection{Far-Field Channel Model}

The RF holographic receiving surface is modeled as an $N_y \times N_z$ uniform planar array (UPA) with $N_{\rm r} = N_y N_z$ elements lying in the $yz$-plane and centered at the origin. With inter-element spacings $d_y$ and $d_z$ along the $y$- and $z$-axes, element $(n_y,n_z)$ ($n_y=1,\ldots,N_y$, $n_z=1,\ldots,N_z$) is located at
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{r}_{n_y,n_z} = \big[0,\, y_{n_y},\, z_{n_z}\big]^T,
\end{equation}
where $y_{n_y} = \big(n_y - \frac{N_y+1}{2}\big)d_y$ and $z_{n_z} = \big(n_z - \frac{N_z+1}{2}\big)d_z$.

Under far-field propagation, the signal from user $l$ arrives as a plane wave with polar angle $\theta_l$ from the array broadside and azimuth angle $\phi_l$ from the positive $y$-axis in the $yz$-plane. With wavenumber $k_0 = 2\pi/\lambda$ (wavelength $\lambda$), the tangential wavenumbers are $k_{l,y} = k_0\sin\theta_l\cos\phi_l$ and $k_{l,z} = k_0\sin\theta_l\sin\phi_l$, yielding the relative phase shift
\begin{equation}
\psi_{n_y,n_z}^{(l)} = k_{l,y}y_{n_y} + k_{l,z}z_{n_z}.
\label{eq:element_phase_shift}
\end{equation}

Assuming constant amplitude across the aperture, path loss, shadowing, center propagation phase, and residual carrier-phase offset relative to the local reference are absorbed into the complex gain $\alpha_l \in \mathbb{C}$. Using the linear element index $i = (n_y-1)N_z + n_z \in \{1,\ldots,N_{\rm r}\}$, the channel vector of user $l$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}_l = \alpha_l \mathbf{v}_l, \qquad [\mathbf{v}_l]_i = e^{j\psi_{n_y,n_z}^{(l)}},
\label{eq:user_channel_vector}
\end{equation}
with array response vector $\mathbf{v}_l \in \mathbb{C}^{N_{\rm r}}$. Stacking all $L$ users gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{H} = [\mathbf{h}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{h}_L] = \mathbf{V}\boldsymbol{\Gamma},
\label{eq:H_V_Gamma}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{V} \triangleq [\mathbf{v}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{v}_L] \in \mathbb{C}^{N_{\rm r} \times L}$ collects the directional array response vectors and $\boldsymbol{\Gamma} \triangleq \operatorname{diag}(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_L) \in \mathbb{C}^{L \times L}$ contains the complex path gains. This factorization separates the angle-dependent spatial steering matrix from the per-user complex gains. We adopt a single dominant far-field path per scheduled user to expose the DOA-dependent parametric structure targeted by our estimator; more general wideband or multipath channels remain compatible with the affine intensity observation model, but require an expanded channel parametrization and are left for future work.


\subsection{Reference-Assisted Holographic Reception Model}

The element-wise reference signals are derived from a common local oscillator
operating at $f_c$ and distributed across the surface through a power-divider,
RF feed lines, and prescribed phase-delay sections
\cite{huangjd2024CS,yin2025hisprototype}. Thus, all reference branches
share the same carrier and maintain stable relative phases over each detection
interval. Fixed amplitude and phase offsets introduced by the distribution
network are assumed to be calibrated and included in the prescribed reference
coefficients. Let $\mathbf x(t)=[x_1(t),\ldots,x_L(t)]^T$. At receiving unit $i$,
the resulting reference is
\begin{equation}
b_i^{\rm RF}(t)
=
\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Re}\!\left\{b_i e^{j2\pi f_ct}\right\},
\qquad b_i=\rho_i e^{j\chi_i},
\label{eq:rf_reference_wave}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_i$ and $\chi_i$ are its prescribed amplitude and phase. Let
$\mathbf b=[b_1,\ldots,b_{N_{\rm r}}]^T$ collect the reference coefficients.
In the spatial four-phase implementation, distinct receiving units are assigned
$\chi_i\in\{0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2\}$ and operate simultaneously. Hence, the four
phases form a calibrated spatial code across the aperture; they are not four
sequential observations of the same field coordinate and incur no additional
symbol intervals.

Under the narrowband model, the received user field has envelope
$[\mathbf H\mathbf x(t)]_i$. Its superposition with
\eqref{eq:rf_reference_wave} gives
\begin{equation}
r_i^{\rm RF}(t)
=
\sqrt{2}\operatorname{Re}\!\left\{
\left([\mathbf H\mathbf x(t)]_i+b_i\right)e^{j2\pi f_ct}
\right\}.
\label{eq:composite_rf_field}
\end{equation}
Here, $\mathbf H\mathbf x(t)$ contains the propagation response and unknown
user phases, whereas $b_i$ is receiver controlled. The reference-assisted
interference is therefore formed in the analog RF domain before square-law
detection and sampling.

Each symbol may be observed over $M$ temporal subslots of duration $\Delta_t = T_s/M$, with centers $t_{k,m} = kT_s + (m+1/2)\Delta_t$ and intervals $\mathcal{I}_{k,m} = [kT_s + m\Delta_t, kT_s + (m+1)\Delta_t)$ for $k = 0,\ldots,K-1$ and $m = 0,\ldots,M-1$. Direct square-law detection and integration are applied to the RF waveform in \eqref{eq:composite_rf_field}. For compactness, denote its composite complex envelope by $u_i(t) \triangleq [\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}(t)]_i + b_i$ for element $i \in \{1,\ldots,N_{\rm r}\}$. Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
z_i[k,m]
&= \frac{1}{\Delta_t}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{k,m}} \bigl[r_i^{\rm RF}(\tau)\bigr]^2\,d\tau + w_i[k,m] \\
&= \frac{1}{\Delta_t}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{k,m}} |u_i(\tau)|^2\,d\tau \\
&\quad + \frac{1}{\Delta_t}\operatorname{Re}\!\left\{ \int_{\mathcal{I}_{k,m}} u_i^2(\tau)e^{j4\pi f_c\tau}\,d\tau \right\} + w_i[k,m] \\
&\simeq \frac{1}{\Delta_t}\int_{\mathcal{I}_{k,m}} |u_i(\tau)|^2\,d\tau + w_i[k,m].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:subslot_energy_baseband}
\end{equation}
After mean-noise-floor calibration, thermal, front-end, detector, and readout impairments are represented by the effective output-noise term $w_i[k,m]$. Because each subslot averages many RF noise samples, $w_i[k,m]$ is approximated as independent zero-mean Gaussian noise with constant variance.

Since $u_i(t)$ varies on the envelope time scale, the double-frequency $2f_c$ term averages out when $\Delta_t f_c \gg 1$, leaving the envelope energy that contains the phase-sensitive cross term $2\operatorname{Re}\{b_i^*[\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}(t)]_i\}$. Thus, \eqref{eq:subslot_energy_baseband} represents RF energy integration rather than coherent downconversion or I/Q sampling. Approximating each subslot average by its midpoint value (adopted as the discrete observation model from this point onward, even when $M=1$) and stacking the $N_{\rm r}$ element outputs yields
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}[k,m]
\simeq
\left|
\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}[k,m] + \mathbf{b}
\right|^2
+
\mathbf{w}[k,m]
\in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\rm r}},
\label{eq:discrete_intensity_measurement}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}[k,m] \triangleq \mathbf{x}(t_{k,m})$ and the squared modulus is taken element-wise. The $MN_{\rm r}$ samples associated with each symbol are retained as a vector. Spatial diversity is supplied by the receiving units and their reference phases, whereas $M>1$ is used only when temporal envelope samples are needed; it is not the number of reference phases.

To relate these energy samples to the transmitted symbols, define $\mathbf D_P=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt{P_1},\ldots,\sqrt{P_L})$ and stack the user symbol vectors into $\mathbf s=[\mathbf s_1^T,\ldots,\mathbf s_L^T]^T \in \mathcal S^{LK}$ (with symbol index $j=0,\ldots,K-1$ within each user). With subslot index $q \triangleq kM+m \in \{0,\ldots,KM-1\}$, define the pulse-shaping matrix $\mathbf P \in \mathbb{R}^{KM \times K}$ with 1-based entries $[\mathbf P]_{q+1,\,j+1}=p(t_{k,m}-jT_s)$. The matrix $\mathbf P_{\rm tx}\triangleq\mathbf D_P\otimes\mathbf P\in \mathbb C^{LKM\times LK}$ maps $\mathbf s$ to the sampled user envelopes in user-major order, where $\otimes$ denotes the Kronecker product.

For general pulse shaping, neighboring symbols may overlap, so the $KMN_{\rm r}$ measurements must be processed jointly. Let $\boldsymbol\Pi \in \mathbb{R}^{LKM \times LKM}$ reorder the user-major envelope $\mathbf P_{\rm tx}\mathbf s$ into subslot-major form (outer index $q = kM+m$ and inner index over users $l=1,\ldots,L$). Stacking \eqref{eq:discrete_intensity_measurement} over all subslots (outer index $q$ and inner receive-element index $i=1,\ldots,N_{\rm r}$) then yields the compact block-level model
\begin{equation}
\mathbf z=|\mathbf G\mathbf s+\mathbf b_{\rm eq}|^2+\mathbf w,
\label{eq:block_level_intensity_model}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf G\triangleq(\mathbf I_{KM}\otimes\mathbf H) \boldsymbol\Pi\mathbf P_{\rm tx}\in\mathbb C^{KMN_{\rm r}\times LK}$ and $\mathbf b_{\rm eq}\triangleq\mathbf 1_{KM}\otimes\mathbf b \in \mathbb{C}^{KMN_{\rm r}}$. Thus, $\mathbf s\in\mathcal S^{LK}$ and $\mathbf z,\mathbf w\in\mathbb R^{KMN_{\rm r}}$. When intersymbol overlap is negligible, \eqref{eq:block_level_intensity_model} decouples into symbol-wise models; otherwise, it describes joint block detection.

\subsection{Two-Phase Transmission Protocol and Observation Model}

Within each frame, a known pilot sequence is first transmitted for channel estimation, followed by a data sequence for finite-alphabet symbol detection using the estimated channel. Both sequences share the same affine-intensity observation structure as \eqref{eq:block_level_intensity_model}.

\subsubsection{Training Phase}
Suppose each user transmits $K_{\rm p}$ pilot symbols, yielding $T_{\rm p} = M K_{\rm p}$ training subslots in total. Let $\mathbf{x}_{\rm p}[q] \in \mathbb{C}^L$ denote the known multiuser baseband waveform sampled at the midpoint of pilot subslot $q = 0,\ldots,T_{\rm p}-1$, and collect them row-wise into the pilot matrix
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{X}_{\rm p} \triangleq \big[\mathbf{x}_{\rm p}[0],\,\ldots,\,\mathbf{x}_{\rm p}[T_{\rm p}-1]\big]^T \in \mathbb{C}^{T_{\rm p} \times L}.
\end{equation}
Define $\mathbf{h} \triangleq \operatorname{vec}(\mathbf{H}) \in \mathbb{C}^{N_{\rm r} L}$. Using the identity $\mathbf{H}\mathbf{x}_{\rm p}[q] = (\mathbf{x}_{\rm p}^T[q] \otimes \mathbf{I}_{N_{\rm r}})\mathbf{h}$ and stacking the $N_{\rm r}$-element intensity observations across all $T_{\rm p}$ pilot subslots gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_{\rm p}
=
\left|
\mathbf{G}_{\rm p}\mathbf{h}
+
\mathbf{b}_{\rm p}
\right|^2
+
\mathbf{w}_{\rm p},
\label{eq:block_pilot_phase_model}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{G}_{\rm p} \triangleq \mathbf{X}_{\rm p} \otimes \mathbf{I}_{N_{\rm r}} \in \mathbb{C}^{N_{\rm r} T_{\rm p} \times N_{\rm r} L}$, and $\mathbf{w}_{\rm p} \in \mathbb{R}^{N_{\rm r} T_{\rm p}}$ stacks the effective output noise. For the fixed spatial reference code considered here, $\mathbf{b}_{\rm p} \triangleq \mathbf{1}_{T_{\rm p}} \otimes \mathbf{b} \in \mathbb{C}^{N_{\rm r} T_{\rm p}}$ repeats the same calibrated $N_{\rm r}$-entry phase map at every pilot subslot.

\subsubsection{Data Transmission Phase}

For a data block of $K_{\rm d}$ symbols per user (spanning $M K_{\rm d}$ subslots and yielding $Q_{\rm d} = N_{\rm r}MK_{\rm d}$ scalar intensity measurements), let $\mathbf s_{\rm d}\in\mathcal S^{D_{\rm s}}$ denote the stacked symbol vector with $D_{\rm s} = LK_{\rm d}$. Analogous to \eqref{eq:block_level_intensity_model}, define $\mathbf G_{\rm d}(\mathbf H)\triangleq (\mathbf I_{MK_{\rm d}}\otimes\mathbf H)\boldsymbol\Pi_{\rm d}\mathbf P_{{\rm tx},{\rm d}}\in\mathbb C^{Q_{\rm d}\times D_{\rm s}}$, where $\mathbf P_{{\rm tx},{\rm d}}$ and $\boldsymbol\Pi_{\rm d}$ are the data-block counterparts of $\mathbf P_{\rm tx}$ and $\boldsymbol\Pi$ for $K_{\rm d}$ symbols per user, and let $\mathbf b_{\rm d}\triangleq \mathbf 1_{MK_{\rm d}}\otimes\mathbf b\in\mathbb C^{Q_{\rm d}}$ collect the calibrated reference across the data subslots. Denote the true noiseless response by $\boldsymbol\mu_{\rm d}(\mathbf s;\mathbf H)\triangleq|\mathbf G_{\rm d}(\mathbf H)\mathbf s+\mathbf b_{\rm d}|^2$. Using the estimated channel $\widehat{\mathbf H}$ and setting $\widehat{\mathbf G}_{\rm d}\triangleq\mathbf G_{\rm d}(\widehat{\mathbf H})$, the receiver forms the detection template $\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}_{\rm d}(\mathbf s)\triangleq|\widehat{\mathbf G}_{\rm d}\mathbf s+\mathbf b_{\rm d}|^2$. The actual observation can therefore be written as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_{\rm d}
=
\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}_{\rm d}(\mathbf s_{\rm d})
+\mathbf e_{\rm CSI}(\mathbf s_{\rm d})
+
\mathbf{w}_{\rm d},
\label{eq:block_data_phase_model}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf e_{\rm CSI}(\mathbf s)\triangleq\boldsymbol\mu_{\rm d}(\mathbf s;\mathbf H)-\widehat{\boldsymbol\mu}_{\rm d}(\mathbf s)$ is the generally symbol-dependent mismatch error caused by imperfect channel state information (CSI) and vanishes for perfect CSI, and $\mathbf w_{\rm d}\in\mathbb R^{Q_{\rm d}}$ is the effective output noise. Thus, the training and data phases share the same affine intensity structure, but the latter performs finite-alphabet detection with an estimated observation map.

Fig.~\ref{fig:frame_sampling} illustrates the subslot-level energy sampling and frame structure of the proposed receiver. On the left, the side view of element $i$ shows the incident RF field combined with the shared-oscillator reference $b_i = \rho_i e^{j\chi_i}$ before square-law detection. Across a symbol interval of duration $T_s$ partitioned into $M$ subslots, each subslot produces a mean-power sample $z_i[k,m]$ around its midpoint, forming an $N_r \times KM$ intensity matrix over a frame of $K = K_{\rm p} + K_{\rm d}$ symbols. The leading $K_{\rm p}$ pilot symbols support channel estimation and the remaining $K_{\rm d}$ payload symbols are used for symbol detection.

\section{Uniqueness and Stability of Reference-Assisted Energy Detection}
\label{sec:uniqueness_stability}

The two-phase receiver described in Section~\ref{sec:system_model} first estimates the complex multiuser channel from known pilots and then detects finite-alphabet data symbols using the estimated channel. Both stages rely on the same reference-assisted intensity measurements. In this section, we first specify the admissible channel parameters and symbol vectors, then establish conditions under which these measurements enable unique and stable channel recovery and distinguish candidate symbol vectors in the presence of noise.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{subsec:problem_formulation}

During training, the far-field channel in \eqref{eq:H_V_Gamma} is specified by the parameter
vector
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\eta}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
\boldsymbol{\theta}^{T} &
\boldsymbol{\phi}^{T} &
\operatorname{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\}^{T} &
\operatorname{Im}\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\}^{T}
\end{bmatrix}^{T}
\in\mathbb{R}^{D_{\eta}},
\label{eq:channel_parameter_vector}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\theta}$, $\boldsymbol{\phi}$, and
$\boldsymbol{\alpha}$ collect the polar angles, azimuth angles, and complex
path gains of the $L$ users, respectively. This structure reduces the number
of real unknowns from $2N_{\rm r}L$ for an unstructured channel to
$D_{\eta}=4L$.

Let $\Omega=\Theta^L\times\Phi^L\times\mathcal A^L$ denote the compact
feasible parameter set, with each complex gain represented by its real and
imaginary parts in $\boldsymbol{\eta}$. The compact angular sets
$\Theta\subseteq[0,\pi/2]$ and $\Phi\subseteq[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$ define the visible
sector, and $\mathcal A=\{\alpha\in\mathbb C:|\alpha|\leq\alpha_{\max}\}$
allows gain magnitudes from zero to $\alpha_{\max}$. Define the stacked
pilot response as
$\mathbf g_{\rm p}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
=\operatorname{col}_t\{\mathbf H(\boldsymbol{\eta})\mathbf x_{\rm p}[t]\}
=\mathbf G_{\rm p}\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf H(\boldsymbol{\eta}))$,
with $T_{\rm p}=MK_{\rm p}$ pilot subslots and $Q_{\rm p}=N_{\rm r}T_{\rm p}$ scalar intensity
measurements. Under the effective Gaussian intensity-noise model, the receiver estimates the channel
parameters by nonlinear least squares:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
=\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\Omega}
F_{\rm p}(\boldsymbol{\eta}),\qquad
F_{\rm p}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\triangleq
\frac{1}{2}
\left\|
\mathbf{z}_{\rm p}
-\left|\mathbf{g}_{\rm p}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
+\mathbf{b}_{\rm p}\right|^{2}
\right\|_{2}^{2}.
\label{eq:joint_doa_channel_objective}
\end{equation}
The training analysis examines when the pilot and reference measurements
distinguish distinct feasible channels and how measurement perturbations
affect the recovery of the angles and gains.

During data transmission, the receiver uses the channel estimate
$\widehat{\mathbf{H}}=\mathbf{H}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}})$ to form
$\widehat{\mathbf{G}}_{\rm d}=\mathbf{G}_{\rm d}(\widehat{\mathbf{H}})$.
The unknown symbol vector satisfies
$\mathbf{s}_{\rm d}\in\mathcal{S}^{D_{\rm s}}$, where $D_{\rm s}=LK_{\rm d}$.
Using the calibrated reference $\mathbf{b}_{\rm d}$, the receiver selects
the candidate whose intensity template in \eqref{eq:block_data_phase_model}
is closest to the observed intensity vector:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathrm{ML}}
=\arg\min_{\mathbf{s}\in\mathcal{S}^{D_{\mathrm{s}}}}
\left\|
\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{d}}
-\left|\widehat{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathrm{d}}\mathbf{s}
+\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{d}}\right|^{2}
\right\|_{2}^{2}.
\label{eq:ml_detector_rewritten}
\end{equation}
Reliable recovery in either phase requires the noiseless intensity
measurements to distinguish distinct feasible channels or symbol vectors.
Even when recovery is unique, small perturbations in the intensity
observations can still cause large channel estimation errors or symbol
detection errors.

\subsection{Unified Affine Phase-Retrieval Model}

To characterize when these ambiguities and errors can arise,
we formulate both recovery problems using a common affine intensity model\cite{affine_phase_retrieval}. The unified model is given by
\begin{equation}
\mathbf z
=
\mathcal T_{\mathbf G,\mathbf b}(\boldsymbol\vartheta)+\mathbf w,
\qquad
\mathcal T_{\mathbf G,\mathbf b}(\boldsymbol\vartheta)
\triangleq
\left|\mathbf G\boldsymbol\vartheta+\mathbf b\right|^2 ,
\label{eq:unified_pr_model}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\mathcal X\subseteq\mathbb C^D$ denotes the generic unknown complex-valued parameter vector, 
$\mathbf G\in\mathbb C^{Q\times D}$ is the measurement matrix, 
$\mathbf b\in\mathbb C^Q$ is the known reference vector, 
and $\mathbf z,\mathbf w\in\mathbb R^Q$ denote the intensity observation and noise vectors, respectively. 
Here, $D$ is the complex dimension of the unknown parameter vector, while $Q$ denotes the total number of scalar intensity measurements. 
The set $\mathcal X$ specifies the feasible domain of $\boldsymbol\vartheta$, and both the modulus and squaring operations are applied element-wise. Thus,
$\mathcal T_{\mathbf G,\mathbf b}$ is the affine magnitude-squared mapping
associated with affine phase retrieval \cite{affine_phase_retrieval}. For
unstructured channel training,
$\boldsymbol\vartheta=\mathbf h=\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf H)$,
$Q=N_{\rm r}T_{\rm p}$, and $D=D_h=N_{\rm r}L$; the physical far-field receiver
restricts this vector to
$\mathcal H_\Omega=\{\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf H(\boldsymbol\eta)):
\boldsymbol\eta\in\Omega\}$ (with real parameter dimension $D_\eta=4L$). In the data phase,
$\boldsymbol\vartheta=\mathbf s_{\rm d}\in\mathcal S^{D_{\rm s}}$,
$Q=Q_{\rm d}$, and $D=D_{\rm s}=LK_{\rm d}$.

Without a reference, a common phase rotation leaves the noiseless
intensity pattern unchanged:
$\mathcal T_{\mathbf G,\mathbf 0}(e^{j\varphi}\boldsymbol\vartheta)
=\mathcal T_{\mathbf G,\mathbf 0}(\boldsymbol\vartheta)$ for any real
$\varphi$. Adding a known reference shifts the field before
square-law detection, giving
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_{\mathbf G,\mathbf b}(\boldsymbol\vartheta)
=
|\mathbf G\boldsymbol\vartheta|^2+|\mathbf b|^2
+2\operatorname{Re}\{\mathbf b^*\odot(\mathbf G\boldsymbol\vartheta)\},
\label{eq:reference_expansion}
\end{equation}
where $(\cdot)^*$ denotes element-wise conjugation and $\odot$ denotes
element-wise multiplication. The term $|\mathbf b|^2$ is a known
offset common to all states. Phase sensitivity comes from the
cross term, which is linear in the real and imaginary parts of
$\boldsymbol\vartheta$. At each detector, the reference phase selects
a real projection of the incident field, and the reference
amplitude sets its weight. This term can therefore distinguish
phase-rotated states that produce identical intensities without
a reference.

For $\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\mathbb C^D$, define the realification
$\boldsymbol\vartheta_{\mathbb R}\triangleq[\operatorname{Re}\{\boldsymbol\vartheta\}^T,
\operatorname{Im}\{\boldsymbol\vartheta\}^T]^T\in\mathbb R^{2D}$. For two
distinct parameters, define the energy-domain distance
\begin{equation}
d_{\rm E}(\boldsymbol\vartheta_1,\boldsymbol\vartheta_2)
\triangleq
\left\|
\mathcal T_{\mathbf G,\mathbf b}(\boldsymbol\vartheta_1)
-
\mathcal T_{\mathbf G,\mathbf b}(\boldsymbol\vartheta_2)
\right\|_2 .
\label{eq:energy_domain_distance_general}
\end{equation}
The optimal lower and upper stability bounds on $\mathcal X$ are
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\mathcal X}
\triangleq
\inf_{\substack{\boldsymbol\vartheta_1,\boldsymbol\vartheta_2\in\mathcal X\\
\boldsymbol\vartheta_1\ne\boldsymbol\vartheta_2}}
\frac{d_{\rm E}(\boldsymbol\vartheta_1,\boldsymbol\vartheta_2)}
{\|\boldsymbol\vartheta_1-\boldsymbol\vartheta_2\|_2},
\qquad
\beta_{\mathcal X}
\triangleq
\sup_{\substack{\boldsymbol\vartheta_1,\boldsymbol\vartheta_2\in\mathcal X\\
\boldsymbol\vartheta_1\ne\boldsymbol\vartheta_2}}
\frac{d_{\rm E}(\boldsymbol\vartheta_1,\boldsymbol\vartheta_2)}
{\|\boldsymbol\vartheta_1-\boldsymbol\vartheta_2\|_2}.
\label{eq:stability_constants}
\end{equation}
Thus, the inverse problem is globally stable on $\mathcal X$ if
$0<\alpha_{\mathcal X}\leq\beta_{\mathcal X}<\infty$.  The lower bound is the
important one: it excludes pairs that are far apart in parameter space but
arbitrarily close in energy space.

\subsection{Exact Secant Identity and Global Uniqueness}

Global uniqueness in classical phase retrieval is formulated as
injectivity modulo a global phase factor
\cite{balan2006signal,bandeira2014saving,conca2015algebraic}.
For the affine intensity map considered here, the quadratic
structure yields an exact identity for the measurement difference
between any two candidate vectors. For a point
$\boldsymbol\vartheta\in\mathbb C^D$, define the real Jacobian
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathbf J(\boldsymbol\vartheta)
\triangleq 2\big[&
\operatorname{Re}\{\operatorname{diag}[
(\mathbf G\boldsymbol\vartheta+\mathbf b)^*]\mathbf G\},\\
&-\operatorname{Im}\{\operatorname{diag}[
(\mathbf G\boldsymbol\vartheta+\mathbf b)^*]\mathbf G\}
\big]\in\mathbb R^{Q\times 2D}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:real_jacobian}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:exact_secant_uniqueness}
For any $\boldsymbol\vartheta_1,\boldsymbol\vartheta_2\in\mathbb C^D$, let
$\boldsymbol\mu=(\boldsymbol\vartheta_1+\boldsymbol\vartheta_2)/2$ and
$\boldsymbol\delta=\boldsymbol\vartheta_1-\boldsymbol\vartheta_2$.  Then
\begin{equation}
\mathcal T_{\mathbf G,\mathbf b}(\boldsymbol\vartheta_1)
-
\mathcal T_{\mathbf G,\mathbf b}(\boldsymbol\vartheta_2)
=
\mathbf J(\boldsymbol\mu)\boldsymbol\delta_{\mathbb R}.
\label{eq:exact_secant_identity}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the mapping $\mathcal T_{\mathbf G,\mathbf b}$ is injective on
$\mathcal X$ if and only if, for every distinct
$\boldsymbol\vartheta_1,\boldsymbol\vartheta_2\in\mathcal X$,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf J\!\left(
\frac{\boldsymbol\vartheta_1+\boldsymbol\vartheta_2}{2}
\right)
(\boldsymbol\vartheta_1-\boldsymbol\vartheta_2)_{\mathbb R}
\ne\mathbf0 .
\label{eq:pairwise_ambiguity_condition_main}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Applying $|a|^2-|c|^2=\operatorname{Re}\{(a+c)^*(a-c)\}$
element-wise gives
$\operatorname{Re}\{[\mathbf G(\boldsymbol\vartheta_1+\boldsymbol\vartheta_2)
+2\mathbf b]^*\odot\mathbf G(\boldsymbol\vartheta_1-\boldsymbol\vartheta_2)\}$,
which equals \eqref{eq:exact_secant_identity} after realification.  Hence, two
distinct points in $\mathcal X$ have identical noiseless intensity observations
exactly when the right-hand side of \eqref{eq:exact_secant_identity} vanishes.
Excluding this possibility for every distinct pair is therefore equivalent to
injectivity on $\mathcal X$, proving \eqref{eq:pairwise_ambiguity_condition_main}.
\end{proof}

For the pair in Theorem~\ref{thm:exact_secant_uniqueness}, the
reference contributes
$2\operatorname{Re}\{\mathbf b^*\odot(\mathbf G\boldsymbol\delta)\}$
to the intensity difference. The field difference
$\mathbf G\boldsymbol\delta$ itself is unchanged. If it vanishes,
the two states are indistinguishable for every reference, so full
column rank of $\mathbf G$ is necessary for injectivity on a
feasible set with nonempty interior. However, even when
$\mathbf G\boldsymbol\delta\neq\mathbf 0$, the two intensity patterns
may still coincide. This occurs precisely when the interference
contribution cancels the unreferenced intensity difference at every
measurement. Avoiding such collisions therefore requires a suitable
choice of the reference vector $\mathbf b$. The following corollary
characterizes the reference vectors that cause these collisions
for a finite set of hypotheses.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:generic_reference_finite_hypotheses}
Let $\Xi=\{\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_{N_{\rm hyp}}\}$ be a finite set of hypotheses, and
let $\mathbf f:\Xi\to\mathbb C^Q$ map each hypothesis to its $Q$
complex field samples across the stacked measurement coordinates before the reference is added. Denote the
resulting field vector for hypothesis $\xi_i$ by
$\mathbf u_i=\mathbf f(\xi_i)$. For $i\ne j$, set
$\mathbf d_{ij}=\mathbf u_i-\mathbf u_j$ and
$\mathbf a_{ij}=(|\mathbf u_i|^2-|\mathbf u_j|^2)/2$. Their $q$-th entries (along the stacked measurement index $q$) are denoted by
$d_{ij,q}=u_{i,q}-u_{j,q}$ and
$a_{ij,q}=(|u_{i,q}|^2-|u_{j,q}|^2)/2$, respectively. A pair with
$\mathbf d_{ij}=\mathbf0$ is indistinguishable for every reference; otherwise,
its ambiguous references form the proper real affine set
\begin{equation}
\mathcal B_{ij}^{\rm bad}
=
\left\{
\mathbf b\in\mathbb C^Q:
\operatorname{Re}\{\mathbf b^*\odot\mathbf d_{ij}\}
=-\mathbf a_{ij}
\right\}.
\label{eq:bad_reference_affine_set}
\end{equation}
If all field codewords are distinct, the finite union
$\bigcup_{i<j}\mathcal B_{ij}^{\rm bad}$ has measure zero in $\mathbb C^Q$;
hence, an absolutely continuously distributed reference makes
$|\mathbf u_i+\mathbf b|^2$ pairwise distinct with probability one.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For two hypotheses in the affine field model, let
$\boldsymbol\mu_{ij}=(\boldsymbol\vartheta_i+\boldsymbol\vartheta_j)/2$ and
$\boldsymbol\delta_{ij}=\boldsymbol\vartheta_i-\boldsymbol\vartheta_j$, so that
$\mathbf u_i=\mathbf G\boldsymbol\vartheta_i$,
$\mathbf u_j=\mathbf G\boldsymbol\vartheta_j$, and
$\mathbf d_{ij}=\mathbf G\boldsymbol\delta_{ij}$.  Expanding the Jacobian--secant
product in \eqref{eq:pairwise_ambiguity_condition_main} gives
\begin{align*}
\mathbf J(\boldsymbol\mu_{ij})
(\boldsymbol\delta_{ij})_{\mathbb R} 
&=2\operatorname{Re}\!\left\{
\left(\frac{\mathbf u_i+\mathbf u_j}{2}+\mathbf b\right)^*
\odot(\mathbf u_i-\mathbf u_j)\right\} \\
&=\operatorname{Re}\!\left\{(\mathbf u_i+\mathbf u_j)^*
\odot\mathbf d_{ij}\right\}
+2\operatorname{Re}\!\left\{\mathbf b^*\odot\mathbf d_{ij}\right\} \\
&=|\mathbf u_i|^2-|\mathbf u_j|^2
+2\operatorname{Re}\!\left\{\mathbf b^*\odot\mathbf d_{ij}\right\} \\
&=2\left(\mathbf a_{ij}
+\operatorname{Re}\!\left\{\mathbf b^*\odot\mathbf d_{ij}\right\}\right).
\end{align*}
The cross terms in the third line cancel after taking the real part.  Hence,
the pair is ambiguous precisely when
$\operatorname{Re}\{\mathbf b^*\odot\mathbf d_{ij}\}=-\mathbf a_{ij}$,
which proves \eqref{eq:bad_reference_affine_set}.  This final identity depends
only on the two field codewords and therefore also holds for a general finite
mapping $\mathbf f$.

For each stacked measurement coordinate $q$, if $d_{ij,q}=0$, then $u_{i,q}=u_{j,q}$ and $a_{ij,q}=0$, so that coordinate
imposes no restriction.  If $d_{ij,q}\ne0$, all ambiguous reference values are
\begin{equation*}
b_q=\left(-\frac{a_{ij,q}}{|d_{ij,q}|^2}+j\tau_q\right)d_{ij,q},
\qquad \tau_q\in\mathbb R,
\end{equation*}
which is one real affine constraint in $\mathbb C$.  Therefore, if
$n_{ij}=|\{q:d_{ij,q}\ne0\}|\geq1$, then
$\mathcal B_{ij}^{\rm bad}$ has real dimension $2Q-n_{ij}<2Q$ and hence
Lebesgue measure zero.  A finite union of such sets remains measure zero,
establishing the almost-sure uniqueness claim.
\end{proof}

Consider a measurement $q$ with reference amplitude $\rho_q>0$ and
field difference $d_{ij,q}\neq 0$. By (24), the two hypotheses produce
the same intensity at this measurement exactly when
$\operatorname{Re}\{b_q^*d_{ij,q}\}=-a_{ij,q}$.
Writing $b_q=\rho_q e^{j\chi_q}$, the conjugate $b_q^*$ contributes
a phase of $-\chi_q$ to the product $b_q^*d_{ij,q}$. Taking the real
part gives the collision condition
\begin{equation}
\rho_q|d_{ij,q}|\cos(\angle d_{ij,q}-\chi_q)=-a_{ij,q}.
\end{equation}
For fixed candidate fields and reference amplitude, this equation
has at most two solutions for $\chi_q$ modulo $2\pi$. Any other
reference phase distinguishes the two hypotheses at measurement $q$,
which suffices to make their intensity vectors distinct. Hence, if the reference support intersects every nonzero field difference, independently and continuously varying phases remove finite-set ambiguities almost surely. For example, under the stated support condition, one can assign $N$
equally spaced reference phases and choose a common phase offset
that avoids all pairwise intensity collisions.

Generic injectivity on a finite set does not by itself ensure a useful noise
margin. Returning to a general feasible set, let
$\mathcal M_{\mathcal X}=\{(\boldsymbol\vartheta_1+
\boldsymbol\vartheta_2)/2:\boldsymbol\vartheta_1,\boldsymbol\vartheta_2\in
\mathcal X\}$ be the midpoint set. The exact identity yields computable
two-sided bounds.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:jacobian_bounds}
Define
\begin{equation}
\underline\sigma_J
\triangleq\inf_{\boldsymbol\mu\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal X}}
\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf J(\boldsymbol\mu)),\qquad
\overline\sigma_J
\triangleq\sup_{\boldsymbol\mu\in\mathcal M_{\mathcal X}}
\sigma_{\max}(\mathbf J(\boldsymbol\mu)).
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
\underline\sigma_J\,
\|\boldsymbol\vartheta_1-\boldsymbol\vartheta_2\|_2
\le d_{\rm E}(\boldsymbol\vartheta_1,\boldsymbol\vartheta_2)
\le
\overline\sigma_J\,
\|\boldsymbol\vartheta_1-\boldsymbol\vartheta_2\|_2
\label{eq:global_bilipschitz_bounds}
\end{equation}
for all pairs $\boldsymbol\vartheta_1,\boldsymbol\vartheta_2\in\mathcal X$. Consequently,
$\alpha_{\mathcal X}\ge\underline\sigma_J$ and
$\beta_{\mathcal X}\le\overline\sigma_J$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply the singular-value inequalities to
\eqref{eq:exact_secant_identity} over the midpoint set.
\end{proof}

The bound $\underline\sigma_J>0$ is a convenient sufficient condition for
global stability, but it can be conservative because it tests every direction
at every midpoint, including directions that may not connect two feasible
points.  The exact constant in \eqref{eq:stability_constants}, or
equivalently the restricted secants in \eqref{eq:exact_secant_identity}, is the
sharp criterion.

The stability constants also depend on the balance between the received field
and the reference wave.  To make this dependence explicit, let
$u_q(\boldsymbol\vartheta)=[\mathbf G\boldsymbol\vartheta]_q$ and, whenever
$u_q(\boldsymbol\vartheta)\ne0$, define the per-measurement reference-to-signal
energy ratio and the corresponding normalized interference depth as
\begin{equation}
\gamma_q(\boldsymbol\vartheta)
\triangleq\frac{|b_q|^2}{|u_q(\boldsymbol\vartheta)|^2},\qquad
\kappa_q(\boldsymbol\vartheta)
\triangleq\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma_q(\boldsymbol\vartheta)}}
{1+\gamma_q(\boldsymbol\vartheta)}.
\label{eq:reference_signal_energy_balance}
\end{equation}
Writing $\Delta\phi_q(\boldsymbol\vartheta)=\angle u_q(\boldsymbol\vartheta)-\angle b_q$, the $q$th noiseless
measurement becomes
\begin{equation}
[\mathcal T_{\mathbf G,\mathbf b}(\boldsymbol\vartheta)]_q
=\bigl(|u_q(\boldsymbol\vartheta)|^2+|b_q|^2\bigr)
\bigl[1+\kappa_q(\boldsymbol\vartheta)\cos\bigl(\Delta\phi_q(\boldsymbol\vartheta)\bigr)\bigr].
\label{eq:normalized_reference_interference}
\end{equation}
Here $0\leq\kappa_q(\boldsymbol\vartheta)\leq1$, with its maximum attained when the two incident
fields have equal energy.  It approaches zero when either field dominates,
showing that the phase-sensitive interference then occupies only a small
fraction of the detected energy.

\subsection{Parametric Channel Uniqueness Requirements}
\label{subsec:parametric_channel_uniqueness}

The affine phase-retrieval result in \cite[Theorem~3.1]{affine_phase_retrieval}
states that a mapping on $\mathbb C^D$ is globally injective if and only if its
real Jacobian has rank $2D$ at every point.  For unstructured channel training,
$D_h=N_{\rm r}L$, $Q_{\rm p}=N_{\rm r}T_{\rm p}$ (with $T_{\rm p}=MK_{\rm p}$ pilot subslots), and the unknown is
$\mathbf h=\operatorname{vec}(\mathbf H)\in\mathbb C^{D_h}$.  The universal
minimum-measurement result in \cite[Theorem~3.2]{affine_phase_retrieval}
therefore gives the necessary condition
\begin{equation}
Q_{\rm p}=N_{\rm r}T_{\rm p}\geq3D_h=3N_{\rm r}L
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad T_{\rm p}\geq3L.
\label{eq:unstructured_global_measurement_count}
\end{equation}
This bound concerns global recovery over the entire space
$\mathbb C^{N_{\rm r}L}$; hence, increasing $N_{\rm r}$ does not reduce the
required number of pilot subslots because it increases the number of unknown
channel coefficients by the same factor.  A generic unconstrained affine
measurement pair is sufficient when $Q_{\rm p}\geq4D_h-1$
\cite[Theorem~3.4]{affine_phase_retrieval}.  

The far-field channel lies on a low-dimensional real parameter manifold. 
Let $d_{\rm a}$ denote the number of angular parameters per user and define
$p=(d_{\rm a}+2)L$ (matching the real parameter dimension $D_{\eta}=4L$ when $d_{\rm a}=2$), where the additional two parameters correspond to the real
and imaginary parts of each complex path gain. 
Define
$
\mathbf h_{\mathbb R}(\boldsymbol\eta)
\triangleq
\left[
\operatorname{Re}\{\mathbf h(\boldsymbol\eta)\}^{T},
\operatorname{Im}\{\mathbf h(\boldsymbol\eta)\}^{T}
\right]^{T},
$
and
$\mathbf H_{\eta}(\boldsymbol\eta)
\triangleq
\partial\mathbf h_{\mathbb R}/\partial\boldsymbol\eta^{T}$.
By the chain rule, the Jacobian with respect to the physical channel parameters is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf J_{\mathrm{p}}(\boldsymbol\eta)
=
\mathbf J_h(\mathbf h(\boldsymbol\eta))
\mathbf H_{\eta}(\boldsymbol\eta)
\in\mathbb R^{Q_{\rm p}\times D_{\eta}},
\label{eq:parametric_jacobian_chain_rule}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf J_h$ is given by \eqref{eq:real_jacobian} with
$\mathbf G=\mathbf G_{\rm p}$ and $\mathbf b=\mathbf b_{\rm p}$.

The pilot waveforms and array response govern how perturbations of
$\boldsymbol\eta$ change the incident field. Through
\eqref{eq:real_jacobian}, the reference $\mathbf b_{\rm p}$ shapes
how these field perturbations appear in the intensity measurements.
A nonzero field perturbation can therefore produce a weak or
vanishing first-order intensity response. Regular local recovery
requires $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{p}}(\boldsymbol{\eta}))=D_{\eta}$. When this
condition holds, $\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{p}}(\boldsymbol{\eta}))$ measures the
weakest local intensity response to a unit parameter perturbation.

For comparison, consider the unstructured full-channel model, for which
$\mathbf G_{\rm p}=\mathbf X_{\rm p}\otimes\mathbf I_{N_{\rm r}}$.
Accordingly,
$\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf G_{\rm p})
=N_{\rm r}\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf X_{\rm p})$
and
$\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf G_{\rm p})
=\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf X_{\rm p})$.
In terms of the number of pilot subslots $T_{\rm p}=MK_{\rm p}$, the
corresponding pilot-length requirements satisfy
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
T_{\rm p}&\geq L
&&\text{for complex-field preservation},\\
T_{\rm p}&\geq 2L
&&\text{for regular local intensity recovery},\\
T_{\rm p}&\geq 3L
&&\text{as a necessary count for global recovery}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:pilot_length_requirement_hierarchy}
\end{equation}
These are dimensional requirements only and do not by themselves ensure
injectivity of the structured pilot--reference map; sufficient pilot and
reference diversity are still required.

\subsection{Finite-Alphabet Uniqueness and Noise Margin}

For data detection, let $\mathcal X_{\rm d}=\mathcal S^{LK_{\rm d}}$. Since
this candidate set is finite, Corollary~\ref{cor:generic_reference_finite_hypotheses}
shows that noiseless uniqueness is equivalent to a positive minimum separation,
defined as
\begin{equation}
d_{\min,{\rm d}}^{(E)}
\triangleq
\min_{\substack{\mathbf s_1,\mathbf s_2\in\mathcal X_{\rm d}\\
\mathbf s_1\ne\mathbf s_2}}
\left\|
|\mathbf G_{\rm d}\mathbf s_1+\mathbf b_{\rm d}|^2
-
|\mathbf G_{\rm d}\mathbf s_2+\mathbf b_{\rm d}|^2
\right\|_2 .
\label{eq:data_energy_domain_min_distance}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:discrete_stability}
The finite-alphabet symbols are uniquely identifiable in the noiseless case if
and only if $d_{\min,{\rm d}}^{(E)}>0$. For an arbitrary additive perturbation,
the nearest-intensity detector returns the transmitted vector whenever
\begin{equation}
\|\mathbf w\|_2<\frac{d_{\min,{\rm d}}^{(E)}}{2}.
\label{eq:bounded_noise_decoding_radius}
\end{equation}
Under exact CSI and the adopted white Gaussian effective output-noise model,
$\mathbf w\sim\mathcal N(\mathbf0,\sigma_w^2\mathbf I)$, the error probability
of the binary ML comparison between distinct candidates $\mathbf s_i$ and
$\mathbf s_j$ is
\begin{equation}
P_{i\to j}
=Q\!\left(\frac{d_E(\mathbf s_i,\mathbf s_j)}{2\sigma_w}\right),
\label{eq:pairwise_error_probability}
\end{equation}
where $Q(x)\triangleq(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int_x^\infty e^{-t^2/2}\,dt$ is the
standard Gaussian tail function.
If $M_{\rm c}=|\mathcal X_{\rm d}|=|\mathcal S|^{LK_{\rm d}}$ is the number
of candidate vectors, the block error probability satisfies
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm B}
\le (M_{\rm c}-1)
Q\!\left(\frac{d_{\min,{\rm d}}^{(E)}}{2\sigma_w}\right).
\label{eq:block_error_union_bound}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\boldsymbol\mu_k=|\mathbf G_{\rm d}\mathbf s_k+\mathbf b_{\rm d}|^2$.
The triangle inequality gives the decoding radius in
\eqref{eq:bounded_noise_decoding_radius}. If $\mathbf s_i$ is transmitted,
the binary ML comparison favors $\mathbf s_j$ only if
$\mathbf w^T(\boldsymbol\mu_j-\boldsymbol\mu_i)\ge
d_E^2(\mathbf s_i,\mathbf s_j)/2$. The left-hand side is Gaussian with
variance $\sigma_w^2d_E^2(\mathbf s_i,\mathbf s_j)$, which gives
\eqref{eq:pairwise_error_probability}. Applying the union bound over the
remaining $M_{\rm c}-1$ candidates and using
$d_E(\mathbf s_i,\mathbf s_j)\ge d_{\min,{\rm d}}^{(E)}$ gives
\eqref{eq:block_error_union_bound}.
\end{proof}

Thus, $d_{\min,{\rm d}}^{(E)}$ provides a common measure of finite-alphabet
identifiability and noise robustness: positivity guarantees noiseless
uniqueness, half of the distance is a deterministic decoding radius, and its
value directly controls the Gaussian error bounds. Because it depends jointly
on $\mathbf G_{\rm d}$, $\mathbf b_{\rm d}$, and the constellation, it also
serves as the reference-design metric and distance certificate used in
Section~\ref{sec:algorithms}.

\subsection{Spatial Four-Phase Coding and Energy Allocation}

The preceding conditions apply to any calibrated reference map. For the
parallel architecture, let each spatial coordinate be assigned one of four
phases,
\begin{equation}
b_q=\rho_qe^{j\chi_q},\qquad
\chi_q\in\{0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2\},\quad \rho_q>0.
\label{eq:spatial_four_phase_code}
\end{equation}
A balanced assignment uses the four states equally often. Since these phases
act on different spatial samples rather than repeated measurements of one
sample, the resulting guarantee is ensemble averaged.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:spatial_four_phase_mean_separation}
Let each phase in \eqref{eq:spatial_four_phase_code} have a uniform marginal
distribution over the four states, independently of a fixed pair
$\boldsymbol\vartheta_1,\boldsymbol\vartheta_2$; mutual independence across spatial
coordinates is not required. Define
$\mathbf u_i=\mathbf G\boldsymbol\vartheta_i$,
$\mathbf c=|\mathbf u_1|^2-|\mathbf u_2|^2$, and
$\Delta\mathbf u=\mathbf u_1-\mathbf u_2$. With
$\rho_{\min}=\min_q\rho_q$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\chi}
\!\left[\|\mathcal T_{\mathbf G,\mathbf b}(\boldsymbol\vartheta_1)
-\mathcal T_{\mathbf G,\mathbf b}(\boldsymbol\vartheta_2)\|_2^2\right]
&=\|\mathbf c\|_2^2+2\sum_q\rho_q^2|\Delta u_q|^2\\
&\ge 2\rho_{\min}^2
\|\mathbf G(\boldsymbol\vartheta_1-\boldsymbol\vartheta_2)\|_2^2.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:spatial_four_phase_mean_distance}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For coordinate $q$, the intensity difference is
$\Delta\mu_q=c_q+2\rho_q
\operatorname{Re}\{e^{-j\chi_q}\Delta u_q\}$. The four-state average satisfies
$\mathbb E_{\chi_q}[\operatorname{Re}\{e^{-j\chi_q}\Delta u_q\}]=0$ and
$\mathbb E_{\chi_q}[\operatorname{Re}^2\{e^{-j\chi_q}\Delta u_q\}]
=|\Delta u_q|^2/2$. Hence,
$\mathbb E_{\chi_q}[\Delta\mu_q^2]
=c_q^2+2\rho_q^2|\Delta u_q|^2$. Summing over $q$, using
$\Delta\mathbf u=\mathbf G(\boldsymbol\vartheta_1-\boldsymbol\vartheta_2)$, and
lower bounding $\rho_q$ by $\rho_{\min}$ proves the result.
\end{proof}

This ensemble identity motivates balanced spatial coding but does not certify
every realized phase map, which must be checked through its actual Jacobian,
restricted secant constant, or finite-alphabet minimum distance.

\begin{corollary}[Pairwise energy allocation under a fixed incident-energy budget]
\label{cor:balanced_energy_allocation}
Under the noise model of Theorem~\ref{thm:discrete_stability}, suppose that
the candidate fields are $\mathbf u_i=\mathbf G_{\rm d}\mathbf s_i$, and define
their average energy and the reference energy per measurement as
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm s}
\triangleq\frac{1}{M_{\rm c}Q_{\rm d}}
\sum_{i=1}^{M_{\rm c}}\|\mathbf u_i\|_2^2, \qquad
P_{\rm b}
\triangleq\frac{1}{Q_{\rm d}}\|\mathbf b_{\rm d}\|_2^2,
\label{eq:average_signal_reference_energy}
\end{equation}
Let $P_{\rm T}=P_{\rm s}+P_{\rm b}$ be fixed and write
$\mathbf u_i=\sqrt{P_{\rm s}}\mathbf v_i$, so that
$(M_{\rm c}Q_{\rm d})^{-1}\sum_i\|\mathbf v_i\|_2^2=1$. If the spatial
phases are uniform over \eqref{eq:spatial_four_phase_code} and
$|b_q|^2=P_{\rm b}$, then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E_{\boldsymbol\chi}[d_{E,ij}^{2}]
&=P_{\rm s}^{2}A_{ij}+2P_{\rm s}P_{\rm b}B_{ij},\\
A_{ij}
&=\bigl\||\mathbf v_i|^2-|\mathbf v_j|^2\bigr\|_2^2,\\
B_{ij}
&=\|\mathbf v_i-\mathbf v_j\|_2^2 .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:pairwise_distance_energy_split}
\end{equation}
For $0\leq A_{ij}<B_{ij}$, this ensemble-mean squared distance is maximized by
\begin{equation}
P_{\rm s}^{\star}
=\frac{B_{ij}}{2B_{ij}-A_{ij}}P_{\rm T},
\qquad
\frac{P_{\rm b}^{\star}}{P_{\rm s}^{\star}}
=1-\frac{A_{ij}}{B_{ij}}.
\label{eq:pairwise_optimal_energy_split}
\end{equation}
In particular, phase-only pairs satisfy $A_{ij}=0$ and are optimally separated
by $P_{\rm s}=P_{\rm b}=P_{\rm T}/2$. Thus, equal energy is a useful nominal
choice for phase-dominated pairs, not a universal optimum; higher-order
constellations and changes in the active pair should be assessed using the
actual $d_{\min,{\rm d}}^{(E)}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let
$a_q=|v_{i,q}|^2-|v_{j,q}|^2$ and
$\delta v_q=v_{i,q}-v_{j,q}$. The noiseless difference is
$\Delta\mu_q=P_{\rm s}a_q
+2\sqrt{P_{\rm s}P_{\rm b}}
\operatorname{Re}\{e^{-j\chi_q}\delta v_q\}$. The four-state moments used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:spatial_four_phase_mean_separation} cancel the cross term and
give \eqref{eq:pairwise_distance_energy_split}. With
$P_{\rm b}=P_{\rm T}-P_{\rm s}$, the objective becomes
$(A_{ij}-2B_{ij})P_{\rm s}^2+2B_{ij}P_{\rm T}P_{\rm s}$; differentiation gives
\eqref{eq:pairwise_optimal_energy_split}, and the second derivative is negative
under the stated condition. For $A_{ij}=0$, the maximizer is the equal-energy
split. A realized code remains governed by its actual $d_{E,ij}$ through
\eqref{eq:pairwise_error_probability}.
\end{proof}

The finite optimum in Corollary~\ref{cor:balanced_energy_allocation} relies on
a fixed total-energy budget; increasing only $P_{\rm b}$ with fixed
$P_{\rm s}$ and noise need not produce an equal-energy optimum. In practice,
pilot or spatial-average energy estimates can tune the local reference to avoid
severe imbalance within the detector dynamic range.


\section{Estimation and Detection Algorithms}
\label{sec:algorithms}

Guided by Section~\ref{sec:uniqueness_stability}, the continuous,
low-dimensional channel parameters are refined by damped Gauss--Newton (GN)
where the intensity Jacobian is well conditioned.  With the channel fixed, the
data symbols form a finite set governed by the energy-domain minimum distance;
maximum-likelihood (ML) detection is the benchmark and a reference-assisted
Gerchberg--Saxton (GS) iteration provides the scalable implementation.

\subsection{Overall two-phase Recovery Framework}
\label{subsec:overall_framework}

The proposed receiver retains the two-phase structure of Section~\ref{sec:system_model}.
The channel parameters, feasible region, and training criterion are defined in
Section~\ref{subsec:problem_formulation}. After Gauss--Newton refinement, the
receiver uses $\widehat{\mathbf H}$ to form
$\widehat{\mathbf G}_{\rm d}$ for the data-phase criterion
in~\eqref{eq:ml_detector_rewritten}. The training reference is designed to
improve the conditioning of $\mathbf J_{\rm p}$, whereas the data reference is
designed to enlarge the finite-alphabet energy distance. The following two
subsections give the corresponding estimators.

\subsection{Joint DOA and Channel-Gain Estimation}
\label{subsec:joint_doa_channel_estimation}

\subsubsection{Nonlinear Least-Squares Criterion}

Joint DOA and channel-gain estimation solves the nonlinear least-squares
problem in~\eqref{eq:joint_doa_channel_objective}. Its parametric Jacobian
exposes weak gains and poorly separated DOAs through small singular values.

\subsubsection{Jacobian of the Parametric Intensity Mapping}

From \eqref{eq:H_V_Gamma}, the field at pilot instant $t$ is
$\mathbf g_{\rm p}[t]=\sum_l\alpha_lx_{{\rm p},l}[t]\mathbf v_l$.
Let $\mathbf v_{\theta,l}=\partial\mathbf v_l/\partial\theta_l$ and
$\mathbf v_{\phi,l}=\partial\mathbf v_l/\partial\phi_l$, obtained directly by
differentiating \eqref{eq:element_phase_shift}. With
$\alpha_{l,R}=\operatorname{Re}\{\alpha_l\}$ and
$\alpha_{l,I}=\operatorname{Im}\{\alpha_l\}$, the four derivative columns for
user $l$ are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:training_field_derivatives}
\frac{\partial\mathbf g_{\rm p}[t]}
{\partial[\theta_l,\phi_l,\alpha_{l,R},\alpha_{l,I}]}
=x_{{\rm p},l}[t]
\bigl[\alpha_l\mathbf v_{\theta,l},\,
\alpha_l\mathbf v_{\phi,l},\,\mathbf v_l,\,j\mathbf v_l\bigr].
\end{equation}
Because $\boldsymbol\eta$ is stacked by parameter type rather than user-by-user,
these four columns for user $l$ occupy column indices $l$, $L+l$, $2L+l$,
and $3L+l$ of $\partial\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{p}}(\boldsymbol{\eta})/\partial\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\mathsf{T}}$,
respectively.

Stack these columns over the pilot instants and let
$\mathbf u_{\rm p}(\boldsymbol\eta)=\mathbf g_{\rm p}(\boldsymbol\eta)+
\mathbf b_{\rm p}$. Specializing the chain rule in
\eqref{eq:parametric_jacobian_chain_rule} gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{p}}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
=
2\operatorname{Re}
\Biggl\{
\operatorname{diag}
\bigl(\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}^{*}(\boldsymbol{\eta})\bigr)\,
\frac{\partial\mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{p}}(\boldsymbol{\eta})}
     {\partial\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\mathsf{T}}}
\Biggr\}
\in
\mathbb{R}^{Q_{\mathrm{p}}\times D_{\eta}}.
\label{eq:parametric_intensity_jacobian_column}
\end{equation}


\subsubsection{Damped Gauss--Newton Update}

At iteration $i$, define $\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}^{(i)} = \mathbf{g}_{\mathrm{p}} \left(
\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(i)} \right) +
\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{p}}$, $\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{p}}^{(i)} =
\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{p}} - \left| \mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}^{(i)}
\right|^{2}$, and \(\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{p}}^{(i)}
=\mathbf{J}_{\mathrm{p}}(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(i)})\) denotes the
intensity-domain Jacobian evaluated at the current iterate.

The damped Gauss--Newton increment is then computed as
\begin{equation}
\Delta\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(i)}
=
\left[
\left(\mathbf{J}_{\rm p}^{(i)}\right)^T
\mathbf{J}_{\rm p}^{(i)}
+
\lambda^{(i)}\mathbf{I}
\right]^{-1}
\left(\mathbf{J}_{\rm p}^{(i)}\right)^T
\mathbf{e}_{\rm p}^{(i)},
\label{eq:parametric_gn_increment}
\end{equation}
Let $\sigma_{\max}^{(i)}$ and $\sigma_{\min}^{(i)}$ be the extreme singular
values of $\mathbf J_{\rm p}^{(i)}$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:jacobian_bounds}
motivates the following damping for a target condition number
$\kappa_{\rm tar}>1$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\lambda_{\rm stab}^{(i)}&=
\left[
\frac{(\sigma_{\max}^{(i)})^2-
\kappa_{\rm tar}(\sigma_{\min}^{(i)})^2}
{\kappa_{\rm tar}-1}
\right]_{+},\\
\lambda^{(i)}&=\max\{\lambda_{\rm LM}^{(i)},\lambda_{\rm stab}^{(i)}\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:stability_aware_gn_damping}
\end{equation}
Here $\lambda_{\rm LM}^{(i)}$ follows the acceptance rule, while the stability
floor caps the condition number of the regularized normal matrix by
$\kappa_{\rm tar}$; it does not alter the rank or conditioning of the underlying
Jacobian. A persistently negligible $\sigma_{\min}^{(i)}$ instead signals
insufficient pilot or reference diversity, which damping cannot restore. The
trial point is
$\boldsymbol\eta_{\rm trial}^{(i+1)}=\Pi_\Omega(
\boldsymbol\eta^{(i)}+\Delta\boldsymbol\eta^{(i)})$, where $\Pi_\Omega$ clips
the angles and radially clips each gain at $\alpha_{\max}$.
\subsubsection{Initialization}

Because \eqref{eq:joint_doa_channel_objective} is nonconvex, we first obtain a
coarse unstructured channel estimate from the calibrated spatial reference.
Neglecting the second-order unknown-field term in the intensity expansion gives
the linearized initializer
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{h}^{(0)}
=
\arg\min_{\mathbf{h}}
\left\|
\mathbf{z}_{\rm p}
-
|\mathbf{b}_{\rm p}|^2
-
2\operatorname{Re}
\left\{
\mathbf{b}_{\rm p}^{*}
\odot
\mathbf{G}_{\rm p}\mathbf{h}
\right\}
\right\|_2^2,
\label{eq:linearized_channel_initialization}
\end{equation}
from which $\mathbf{H}^{(0)}$ is reconstructed. Although this approximation is
most accurate when the reference term dominates locally, the subsequent
damped nonlinear iterations use the complete intensity model.

Each initial DOA maximizes the normalized matched-filter score
$|\mathbf v^H(\theta,\phi)\mathbf h_l^{(0)}|^2/
\|\mathbf v(\theta,\phi)\|_2^2$ over a coarse grid
$\mathcal G_\Omega$; the corresponding gain follows by least-squares
projection onto the steering vector. These parameters initialize the damped
Gauss--Newton iterations.
The complete joint estimation procedure is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:joint_gn_estimation}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Joint DOA and Channel-Gain Estimation}
\label{alg:joint_gn_estimation}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $\mathbf z_{\rm p}$, $\mathbf x_{\rm p}[t]$, $\mathbf b_{\rm p}$, receiving-surface geometry,
$\Omega$, $\mathcal G_{\Omega}$, $I_{\rm c}$,
$\lambda_{\rm LM}^{(0)}$, $\kappa_{\rm tar}$, and
$\epsilon_{\rm c},\epsilon_0>0$.
\ENSURE Estimated parameter vector $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}$ and channel $\widehat{\mathbf{H}}$.

\STATE Obtain $\mathbf{H}^{(0)}$ from the linearized spatial-code initializer in~\eqref{eq:linearized_channel_initialization}.
\STATE Obtain coarse DOAs using the grid search described above.
\STATE Form $\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(0)}$ according to~\eqref{eq:channel_parameter_vector}.

\FOR{$i=0,1,\ldots,I_{\rm c}-1$}
\STATE Compute $\mathbf{u}_{\rm p}^{(i)} = \mathbf{g}_{\rm p}\bigl(\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(i)}\bigr) + \mathbf{b}_{\rm p}$.
\STATE Compute the residual $\mathbf{e}_{\rm p}^{(i)} = \mathbf{z}_{\rm p} - \left|\mathbf{u}_{\rm p}^{(i)}\right|^{2}$.
\STATE Construct $\mathbf{J}_{\rm p}^{(i)}$ using~\eqref{eq:parametric_intensity_jacobian_column}.
\STATE Set $\lambda^{(i)}$ using the stability floor in~\eqref{eq:stability_aware_gn_damping}.
\STATE Compute $\Delta\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(i)}$ using~\eqref{eq:parametric_gn_increment}.
\STATE Compute $\boldsymbol\eta_{\rm trial}^{(i+1)}=
\Pi_\Omega(\boldsymbol\eta^{(i)}+\Delta\boldsymbol\eta^{(i)})$.
\IF{the trial point decreases $F_{\rm p}$}
\STATE Accept $\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(i+1)} = \boldsymbol{\eta}_{\rm trial}^{(i+1)}$.
\STATE Set $\lambda_{\rm LM}^{(i+1)}=\lambda_{\rm LM}^{(i)}/3$.
\ELSE
\STATE Set $\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(i+1)} = \boldsymbol{\eta}^{(i)}$.
\STATE Set $\lambda_{\rm LM}^{(i+1)}=10\lambda_{\rm LM}^{(i)}$.
\ENDIF
\IF{$\|\Delta\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(i)}\|_{2} \big/ \bigl(\|\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(i)}\|_{2}+\epsilon_0\bigr) < \epsilon_{\rm c}$}
\STATE \textbf{break}
\ENDIF
\ENDFOR

\STATE Set $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}} = \boldsymbol{\eta}^{(i+1)}$.
\STATE Reconstruct $\widehat{\mathbf{H}} = \mathbf{H}\bigl(\widehat{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\bigr)$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\subsection{Finite-Alphabet Symbol Detection}
\label{subsec:symbol_detection}

After training, $\widehat{\mathbf H}$ is used to construct
$\widehat{\mathbf G}_{\rm d}$ in \eqref{eq:block_data_phase_model}. Because
$\mathbf s_{\rm d}\in\mathcal S^{D_{\rm s}}$ is finite-alphabet, reliability
is governed by the energy-domain separation in
\eqref{eq:data_energy_domain_min_distance}, rather than by a continuous
Jacobian rank condition.

\subsubsection{Maximum-Likelihood Benchmark}

The nearest-intensity comparison in~\eqref{eq:ml_detector_rewritten} is the
perfect-CSI ML detector and serves as its plug-in counterpart under estimated
CSI.

Replacing $\mathbf G_{\rm d}$ in~\eqref{eq:data_energy_domain_min_distance} by
$\widehat{\mathbf G}_{\rm d}$ gives the estimated margin.  Exhaustive ML still
evaluates $|\mathcal S|^{D_{\rm s}}$ candidates and is mainly a short-block
benchmark.

\subsubsection{Linearized Initialization and Candidate Screening}

If the reference-induced interference dominates the unknown self-intensity,
$\mathbf z_{\rm d}-|\mathbf b_{\rm d}|^2\approx\mathbf A_{\rm d}
\bar{\mathbf s}_{\rm d}$, where
$\bar{\mathbf s}=[\operatorname{Re}\{\mathbf s\}^T,
\operatorname{Im}\{\mathbf s\}^T]^T\in\mathbb R^{2D_{\rm s}}$ and
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{A}_{\rm d}
=
2
\begin{bmatrix}
\operatorname{Re}\{\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{b}_{\rm d}^{*})
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}_{\rm d}\}&
-\operatorname{Im}\{\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{b}_{\rm d}^{*})
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}_{\rm d}\}
\end{bmatrix}
\in\mathbb R^{Q_{\rm d}\times 2D_{\rm s}}.
\label{eq:linearized_data_matrix}
\end{equation}

A regularized real-domain initialization is
\begin{equation}
\bar{\mathbf{s}}_{\rm lin}
=
\left(
\mathbf{A}_{\rm d}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{\rm d}
+
\gamma_{\rm lin}\mathbf{I}_{2D_{\rm s}}
\right)^{-1}
\mathbf{A}_{\rm d}^{T}
\left(
\mathbf{z}_{\rm d}
-
|\mathbf{b}_{\rm d}|^2
\right),
\label{eq:linearized_data_estimate}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_{\rm lin}\geq0$ (with the inverse understood as a pseudoinverse
$(\mathbf{A}_{\rm d}^{T}\mathbf{A}_{\rm d})^{\dagger}\mathbf{A}_{\rm d}^{T}$
when $\gamma_{\rm lin}=0$ and $\mathbf{A}_{\rm d}$ is not full column rank;
when $\gamma_{\rm lin}=0$ and $\mathbf{A}_{\rm d}$ has full column rank, the
ordinary inverse applies). The complex estimate
$\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf I_{D_{\rm s}} & j\mathbf I_{D_{\rm s}}\end{bmatrix}\bar{\mathbf s}_{\rm lin}$
is projected element-wise onto $\mathcal S$ and used either to initialize an
iterative detector or to center a reduced ML candidate list.

Let $0<\underline d_{\rm d}^{(E)}\leq\widehat d_{\min,{\rm d}}^{(E)}$ be a
certified bound obtained for the realized reference map from
\eqref{eq:data_energy_domain_min_distance} or another valid restricted-distance
bound. A candidate
$\mathbf s_{\rm c}$ is the unique ML solution whenever
\begin{equation}
r(\mathbf s_{\rm c})\triangleq
\left\|\mathbf z_{\rm d}-
|\widehat{\mathbf G}_{\rm d}\mathbf s_{\rm c}+\mathbf b_{\rm d}|^2
\right\|_2
<\frac{\underline d_{\rm d}^{(E)}}{2}.
\label{eq:distance_certified_ml_test}
\end{equation}
Every other candidate then has residual at least
$\underline d_{\rm d}^{(E)}-r(\mathbf s_{\rm c})>r(\mathbf s_{\rm c})$ by the
triangle inequality.  The test is therefore applied to the linearized candidate
and subsequently to the GS iterates.

\subsubsection{Reference-Assisted Gerchberg--Saxton Detection}

GS alternates between the measured magnitudes and the finite-alphabet signal
set while explicitly including the reference in the forward field. Let
$\mathbf r_{\rm d}=\sqrt{[\mathbf z_{\rm d}]_+}$, where $[\cdot]_+$ is applied
element-wise, and define the regularized back-projection
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{d}}
=
\bigl(
\hat{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathrm{d}}^{\mathsf{H}}
\hat{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathrm{d}}
+
\gamma\mathbf{I}
\bigr)^{-1}
\hat{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathrm{d}}^{\mathsf{H}},
\label{eq:regularized_back_projection}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma\geq0$; for $\gamma=0$ and full column rank,
$\mathbf B_{\rm d}=\hat{\mathbf G}_{\rm d}^{\dagger}$. One GS iteration is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf u_{\rm d}^{(i)}&=\hat{\mathbf G}_{\rm d}\mathbf s^{(i)}+\mathbf b_{\rm d},\\
\mathbf s^{(i+1)}&=\mathcal Q_{\mathcal S}\!\left(
\mathbf B_{\rm d}\bigl[\mathbf r_{\rm d}\odot
e^{j\angle\mathbf u_{\rm d}^{(i)}}-\mathbf b_{\rm d}\bigr]\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:reference_assisted_gs_update}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal Q_{\mathcal S}$ is element-wise nearest-neighbor quantization;
the complete procedure is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:reference_assisted_gs}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Reference-Assisted Gerchberg--Saxton Symbol Detection}
\label{alg:reference_assisted_gs}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE $\mathbf z_{\rm d}$, $\widehat{\mathbf G}_{\rm d}$, $\mathbf b_{\rm d}$,
$\mathcal S$, $\underline d_{\rm d}^{(E)}$, $\gamma$, $I_{\rm d}$, and
$\epsilon_{\rm d},\epsilon_0>0$.
\ENSURE Detected symbol vector $\widehat{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathrm{d}}$.
\STATE Compute $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{d}}
= \sqrt{[\mathbf{z}_{\mathrm{d}}]_{+}}$.
\STATE Precompute $\mathbf{B}_{\mathrm{d}}$ according
to~\eqref{eq:regularized_back_projection}.
\STATE Initialize $\mathbf{s}^{(0)}$ using a linearized
estimate, the previous data block, or a random constellation vector.
\FOR{$i=0,1,\ldots,I_{\mathrm{d}}-1$}
    \STATE Compute $\mathbf u_{\rm d}^{(i)}=
    \widehat{\mathbf G}_{\rm d}\mathbf s^{(i)}+\mathbf b_{\rm d}$.
    \STATE Evaluate $r(\mathbf s^{(i)})$ in~\eqref{eq:distance_certified_ml_test}.
    \IF{$r(\mathbf s^{(i)})<\underline d_{\rm d}^{(E)}/2$}
        \STATE Output $\widehat{\mathbf s}_{\rm d}=\mathbf s^{(i)}$ and terminate.
    \ENDIF
    \STATE Set $\tilde{\mathbf u}_{\rm d}^{(i)}=
    \mathbf r_{\rm d}\odot e^{j\angle\mathbf u_{\rm d}^{(i)}}$.
    \STATE Set $\tilde{\mathbf s}^{(i+1)}=
    \mathbf B_{\rm d}(\tilde{\mathbf u}_{\rm d}^{(i)}-\mathbf b_{\rm d})$.
    \STATE Set $\mathbf s^{(i+1)}=
    \mathcal Q_{\mathcal S}(\tilde{\mathbf s}^{(i+1)})$.
    \IF{$
    \|\mathbf{s}^{(i+1)}-\mathbf{s}^{(i)}\|_{2}
    /
    \bigl(\|\mathbf{s}^{(i)}\|_{2}+\epsilon_0\bigr)
    < \epsilon_{\mathrm{d}}
    $}
        \STATE \textbf{break}
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE Output
$\widehat{\mathbf{s}}_{\mathrm{d}}
= \mathcal{Q}_{\mathcal{S}}\bigl(\mathbf{s}^{(i+1)}\bigr)$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

$\mathbf B_{\rm d}$ and the distance bound are precomputed per coherence block;
well-separated blocks can therefore terminate after few GS iterations.

As an iterative baseline, projected Wirtinger flow (WF)~\cite{candes2015wirtinger} applies gradient steps
to the same affine intensity loss, projects the continuous iterate onto the
constellation convex hull, and uses final nearest-neighbor quantization. It uses
the same linearized initialization and distance certificate as GS.

\subsection{Cramér--Rao Lower Bound for DOA Estimation}
\label{subsec:crlb_doa}

The CRLB follows directly from the parameterized training model
in~\eqref{eq:block_pilot_phase_model} and its Jacobian
in~\eqref{eq:parametric_intensity_jacobian_column}. Under the effective
intensity-domain approximation introduced in Section~\ref{sec:system_model},
assume that the noise is white Gaussian,
$\mathbf{w}_{\rm p}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\sigma_{\rm p}^{2}\mathbf{I})$,
with variance independent of the unknown parameters. Repartition the real
parameter vector in~\eqref{eq:channel_parameter_vector} as
$\boldsymbol{\eta}=[\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\mathsf T},
\boldsymbol{\nu}^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}$, where
$\boldsymbol{\psi}=[\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\mathsf T},
\boldsymbol{\phi}^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}$ contains the DOAs and
$\boldsymbol{\nu}=[\operatorname{Re}\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\}^{\mathsf T},
\operatorname{Im}\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}\}^{\mathsf T}]^{\mathsf T}$ contains
the nuisance path gains. The Fisher information matrix (FIM) is then
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
=
\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm p}^{2}}
\mathbf{J}_{\rm p}^{\mathsf T}(\boldsymbol{\eta})
\mathbf{J}_{\rm p}(\boldsymbol{\eta}),
\label{eq:fim_training}
\end{equation}
with the column partition
$\mathbf{J}_{\rm p}=[\mathbf{J}_{\psi}\ \mathbf{J}_{\nu}]$. Eliminating the
unknown gains gives the equivalent FIM for the DOAs,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F}_{\rm DOA}
=\mathbf{F}_{\psi\psi}
-\mathbf{F}_{\psi\nu}
\mathbf{F}_{\nu\nu}^{\dagger}
\mathbf{F}_{\nu\psi}
=\frac{1}{\sigma_{\rm p}^{2}}
\mathbf{J}_{\psi}^{\mathsf T}
\mathbf{P}_{\nu}^{\perp}
\mathbf{J}_{\psi},
\label{eq:equivalent_fim_doa}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{P}_{\nu}^{\perp}=\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{J}_{\nu}\mathbf{J}_{\nu}^{\dagger}$, and $(\cdot)^{\dagger}$ denotes the Moore--Penrose inverse. Thus, only the
component of an angle-induced intensity perturbation orthogonal to the gain
perturbation subspace carries DOA information. If $\mathbf{F}_{\rm DOA}$ is
nonsingular, any unbiased DOA estimator satisfies
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Cov}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\psi}})
\succeq
\mathbf{F}_{\rm DOA}^{-1}.
\label{eq:crlb_doa}
\end{equation}
The diagonal entries give the individual bounds for $\theta_l$ and $\phi_l$.
Thus, weak or rank-deficient directions of
$\mathbf P_\nu^\perp\mathbf J_\psi$ respectively produce large or unbounded
DOA limits, linking reference design directly to attainable angular accuracy.


\section{Simulation Results}

Numerical results first validate the identifiability and accuracy limits in
Section~\ref{sec:uniqueness_stability}, and then examine how the resulting
stability and distance criteria improve the algorithms in
Section~\ref{sec:algorithms}. The remaining experiments examine angular
separation and reference--signal energy balance.

Unless otherwise stated, the simulations follow the two-phase model in
Section~\ref{sec:system_model} with a two-user $8\times8$ RF holographic receiving surface
operating at $3.5$~GHz and half-wavelength element spacing. The complex path
gains are $\alpha_1=e^{j0.35}$ and
$\alpha_2=0.85e^{-j0.55}$, normalized QPSK is used, and the reference amplitude
is $1.5$. Each fixed spatial reference map assigns phases from
$\{0,\pi/2,\pi,3\pi/2\}$ across the receiving units and is reused over time;
one energy integration is taken per symbol ($M=1$). Training and data observations follow
\eqref{eq:block_pilot_phase_model} and~\eqref{eq:block_data_phase_model} with
independent real Gaussian effective output noise of variance $\sigma_w^2$ per
intensity sample. For a noiseless intensity vector
$\boldsymbol{\mu}=|\mathbf{G}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}+\mathbf{b}|^2\in
\mathbb{R}^{Q}$, the SNR is defined as
\begin{equation}
{\rm SNR}
=
10\log_{10}
\left(
\frac{
\mathbb{E}\{\|\boldsymbol{\mu}\|_2^2\}
}{
Q\sigma_w^2
}
\right).
\label{eq:simulation_snr_definition}
\end{equation}
Here, the expectation is evaluated over the noiseless samples of the
corresponding experiment and, in the data phase, over equiprobable transmitted
symbol vectors.
Scenario-specific DOAs, pilot lengths, and reference or surface configurations are
stated with the corresponding experiments.

\subsection{Channel Estimation Performance}

For $N_{\rm MC}$ Monte Carlo trials, the reported channel normalized
mean-square error is
\begin{equation}
{\rm NMSE}_{\mathbf H}
=\frac{1}{N_{\rm MC}}
\sum_{r=1}^{N_{\rm MC}}
\frac{\|\widehat{\mathbf H}^{(r)}-\mathbf H\|_{\rm F}^{2}}
{\|\mathbf H\|_{\rm F}^{2}}.
\end{equation}
Its value in decibels is
${\rm NMSE}_{\mathbf H,{\rm dB}}=10\log_{10}{\rm NMSE}_{\mathbf H}$.
Let
$\boldsymbol{\psi}_l=[\theta_l,\phi_l]^T$ and
$\widehat{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_l^{(r)}$ denote the true and estimated angular
vectors of user $l$ in degrees. The reported DOA RMSE averages all $2L$
angular components:
\begin{equation}
{\rm RMSE}_{\rm DOA}
=
\left[
\frac{1}{2N_{\rm MC}L}
\sum_{r=1}^{N_{\rm MC}}
\sum_{l=1}^{L}
\left\|
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_l^{(r)}-\boldsymbol{\psi}_l
\right\|_2^2
\right]^{1/2}.
\label{eq:doa_rmse_definition}
\end{equation}
The root CRLB uses the same aggregation. With $T_{\rm p}=32$ QPSK pilots and
user DOAs $(15^{\circ},8^{\circ})$ and $(35^{\circ},-10^{\circ})$,
Fig.~\ref{fig:doa_rmse_crlb_snr} shows that the RMSE decreases from
$1.04^{\circ}$ at $0$~dB to $0.060^{\circ}$ at $24$~dB and closely follows
the CRLB over $1000$ noise realizations. Because the constrained estimator may
be biased at finite SNR, the CRLB is used as a local accuracy benchmark.

\subsection{Symbol Detection Performance with Perfect CSI}

For $N_{\rm MC}$ trials, the symbol error rate used throughout the following
experiments is
\begin{equation*}
{\rm SER}
=\frac{1}{N_{\rm MC}LK_{\rm d}}
\sum_{r=1}^{N_{\rm MC}}
\sum_{l=1}^{L}
\sum_{k=1}^{K_{\rm d}}
\mathbf 1\!\left\{
\widehat s_{l,k}^{(r)}\ne s_{l,k}^{(r)}
\right\}.
\end{equation*}
We isolate the detector from channel-estimation errors by constructing
$\mathbf G_{\rm d}$ from the true channel. Two QPSK users and the simultaneous
spatial four-phase map with $\rho=1.5$ are used.
Figure~\ref{fig:certified_gs_accuracy_complexity}(a) compares the detectors
over $30{,}000$ symbol-vector trials per point. At $-5$~dB, the ML,
projected-WF, and certified-GS SERs are $0.164$, $0.165$, and $0.214$,
respectively. The distance-spectrum union bound derived from
Theorem~\ref{thm:discrete_stability} approaches ML in the waterfall region.

Figure~\ref{fig:certified_gs_accuracy_complexity}(b) evaluates the stopping
test in~\eqref{eq:distance_certified_ml_test} using the same trials.
At low SNR the certificate is deliberately inactive and both iterative methods
approach the $25$-iteration budget; at $11$~dB, their averages fall to $3.80$
for projected WF and $4.13$ for GS. Projected WF additionally requires a
gradient evaluation and backtracking at each iteration.

\subsection{End-to-End Symbol Detection with Estimated CSI}

We next propagate the preceding channel estimates to exhaustive data detection
at $0$~dB data SNR. Each of $300$ training-noise realizations uses $200$ data
vectors. The fixed-short, stability-guided, and fixed-long receivers differ
only in their pilot prefixes, while the perfect-CSI curve gives the data-noise
floor.

At low training SNR, the stability rule requests the long prefix and closely
tracks fixed-long training. At $0$~dB, it lowers the SER from about $0.18$ with
eight pilots to about $0.14$ and improves the channel NMSE from $-14.0$ to
$-20.9$~dB. At higher training SNR, the selected prefix shortens and the
estimated-CSI curves approach the perfect-CSI floor, confirming that the gain
comes from controlling channel-error propagation.

\subsection{Effect of the Reference-to-Signal Energy Ratio}

We finally examine the energy allocation in
Corollary~\ref{cor:balanced_energy_allocation}. The total average incident energy
per unit is fixed as $P_{\rm s}+P_{\rm b}=1$, so varying
${\rm RSR}=10\log_{10}(P_{\rm b}/P_{\rm s})$ changes only the allocation.
Figure~\ref{fig:reference_signal_energy_ratio} averages $1500$ trials over each
of $24$ phase-balanced fixed maps; the noise variance is calibrated at
${\rm RSR}=0$ and held fixed over the sweep.

Figure~\ref{fig:reference_signal_energy_ratio}(a) shows that the ensemble-mean
minimum distance is maximized near balance, at the sampled value

\eqref{eq:pairwise_optimal_energy_split}, whose optimum depends on
$A_{ij}/B_{ij}$, and the SER curves exhibit the corresponding bowl shape.
Thus, severe imbalance weakens phase-sensitive separation, while the preferred
ratio remains scenario dependent.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper developed a reference-assisted RF holographic receiver for recovering complex channel and data information from intensity-only measurements. For the resulting affine intensity model, we established Jacobian-based uniqueness and stability conditions for continuous channel sensing and an energy-domain minimum-distance criterion for finite-alphabet detection. The analysis further revealed the roles of pilot diversity, spatial four-phase reference coding, and reference--signal energy balance. Guided by these results, we designed a two-phase receiver combining linearized initialization, stability-guided adaptive training, damped Gauss--Newton estimation, and distance-certified GS detection. Simulations under representative two-user far-field settings supported the predicted trends and showed that the adaptive scheme reduced the average pilot length from $40$ to $20.0$ at $10$~dB while attaining $-28.1$~dB channel NMSE. These results demonstrate the feasibility of recovering complex communication information without coherent I/Q acquisition.
